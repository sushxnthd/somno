## 1. Somno is a wellness tool, not a medical device

This is the most important term here, so it comes first.

Somno estimates how alert you are from a reaction-time task, a camera-based measurement of your
face, your own sleepiness rating, and the sleep you have logged. Those estimates are **modelled**,
not measured clinically:

- The alertness index is a fusion of the four signals above, scored against your own history.
- The sleep-stage breakdown and the hypnogram are **simulations** driven by your logged duration and
  score. Without a wearable, no app can know when you were in REM, and Somno does not claim to.
- Smart Wake moves an alarm earlier to a predicted lighter phase of sleep. It is a prediction.
- The eyelid measurement is a proxy for eyelid closure, not the clinical PERCLOS measure, and the
  app says so where it is shown.

**Somno does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any medical condition**, and it is not a substitute
for professional advice. Nothing in it is clinically validated.

**Do not use Somno to decide whether you are safe to drive**, operate machinery, or do anything else
where being wrong could hurt you or somebody else. A score that says you are alert is not permission
to drive tired. If you are persistently sleepy, or think you may have a sleep disorder, talk to a
doctor.

## 2. The alarm

Somno includes an alarm clock, and it is built to be reliable: it uses the device's exact-alarm and
full-screen-intent facilities, re-arms itself after each firing and after a reboot, and the volume
escalates until you respond.

Even so, **do not rely on it alone for anything critical.** Every alarm on every phone depends on
things outside an app's control: battery optimisation and "deep sleep" modes on some manufacturers'
software, Do Not Disturb, silent or headphone-routed audio, a flat battery, a powered-off phone, or
the operating system revoking the exact-alarm permission. If missing a wake-up would be seriously
costly, set a second alarm.

Where the device does not permit exact alarms, Somno tells you so on the alarms screen rather than
degrading silently.
